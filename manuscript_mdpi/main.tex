\abstract{Author-stated research gaps could support automated idea generation, but they are sparse and scattered. We present a three-stage funnel: rules remove unlikely text, a zero-shot natural-language-inference classifier selects candidates, and a large language model (LLM) filters them without rewriting. One instruction selects high-precision, balanced or high-recall filtering. We evaluate on two human-labelled benchmarks, SAL limitations and FWS future-work statements. Because each covers one gap type in one field, we also built a corpus of 230 arXiv and biomedical papers labelled with LLMs for three gap types. In high-precision mode, the filter raised SAL precision from 29.1\% after candidate selection to 66.2\%, at 84.9\% recall; the three modes ordered precision and recall as intended on every dataset. With a 27B model, the funnel's observed \texorpdfstring{$F_1$}{F1} exceeded whole-paper prompting on SAL (0.744 versus 0.702; the paired interval included zero) and FWS while using about one-third of the input tokens; on arXiv, whose labels came from whole-paper inputs, whole-paper prompting led. With 9B and 4B models, whole-paper \texorpdfstring{$F_1$}{F1} on arXiv fell to 0.313 and 0.296, versus 0.599 and 0.544 for the funnel. Results are provisional: no person has checked the corpus labels, and some filter settings were developed on its test papers.}
