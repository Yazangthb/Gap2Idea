\abstract{Limitations, future work, and open problems stated by authors are research gaps that could seed automated idea generation, but extracting them at scale requires source attribution and controlled cost. We study a three-stage funnel---structural localization, a sentence classifier, and a large-language-model (LLM) precision filter applied only to the remaining candidates---that returns source sentences rather than generated text. We evaluate it on 40 biomedical randomized-trial articles with published limitation labels and on a 230-article arXiv and biomedical corpus (68,719 sentences, 4462 gap sentences) labelled by an LLM ensemble ($\kappa = 0.78$). On the trial articles, localization discards 76.3\% of sentences while keeping 203 of 205 limitations; a fine-tuned PubMedBERT classifier reaches $F_1$ 0.604, and the LLM filter raises precision from 0.460 to 0.580 ($F_1$ 0.658) with an estimated 3.74 times fewer input tokens than full-article prompting. On the cross-domain corpus, a zero-shot natural-language-inference cross-encoder reaches $F_1$ 0.617 without training, outperforming fine-tuned and frozen encoders, and a recall-first configuration keeps 96.5\% of gaps while discarding 38\% of sentences. Section recovery does not raise arXiv localization recall because gaps are dispersed. LLM results are exploratory, and ensemble labels await human validation. The funnel offers a measured, attributable precision--cost trade-off towards automated idea generation.}
