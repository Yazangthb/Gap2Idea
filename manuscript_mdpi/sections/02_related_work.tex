\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

Gap2Idea builds on research into scientific discourse classification, the recognition of author-stated limitations and future work, and LLM-based analysis of scholarly documents. Across these areas, similar terminology can refer to different tasks: locating an existing statement, inferring a missing piece of knowledge, or generating a new criticism or research direction. We organise the discussion around these distinctions and explain how each line of work informs our funnel. Table~\ref{tab:related} compares the closest studies by target, method and input unit.

\subsection{Scientific discourse and sentence classification}

Scientific discourse annotation provides a foundation for identifying statements by their role within a paper. Argumentative zoning represents the rhetorical function of sentences and their relationship to the authors' argument~\cite{teufel2002summarizing}. Liakata et al.~\cite{liakata2010corpora} distinguish this rhetorical perspective from Core Scientific Concepts (CoreSC), which represent components of an investigation, including its goals, methods, observations and conclusions. The Dr.~Inventor corpus brings the discussion closer to our targets: its 40 computer-graphics articles have a Future Work category in the argumentative-structure layer and a Limitation category among the crosswise features~\cite{fisas2016multilayered}. These annotations establish a full-text precedent for representing both kinds of statement, although they do not constitute a single research-gap detection task.

Abstract sentence classification supplies further resources and modelling approaches. PubMed 200k RCT and CSAbstruct support the recognition of abstract roles in biomedical and computer-science publications, respectively~\cite{dernoncourt2017pubmed,cohan2019pretrained}. MAZEA allows multiple rhetorical labels per sentence~\cite{dayrell2012rhetorical}, while Emerald~110k extends coverage across disciplines and includes a combined Research limitations/implications heading~\cite{stead2019emerald}. Such resources provide useful discourse supervision, but their labels and document scope differ from ours: a conclusion sentence need not state a gap, and a combined limitations/implications label also covers other content.

More targeted rhetorical schemes make the overlap explicit. Sci-Sentence includes Research Gap and Limitation among seven categories intended to support literature-review analysis~\cite{bolanos2025scisentence}. Its benchmark samples sentences from introductions, related-work sections and limitations sections. Its Research Gap category overlaps with our open-problem target, whereas its Limitation definition concerns weaknesses of a previous study rather than exclusively the authors' own work. This difference in attribution matters when deciding which statements count as positives.

Context is another shared concern. Hierarchical sentence classifiers model surrounding discourse~\cite{jin2018hierarchical}, and Brack et al.~\cite{brack2024sequential} investigate cross-domain sequential classification through multi-task learning. Lan et al.~\cite{lan2024multilabel} extend sequential classification to LLMs and multi-label outputs, while Zhang and Zhang~\cite{zhang2024extracting} use context-enhanced representations to identify problem and method sentences. These studies motivate considering neighbouring text when a sentence is ambiguous. In Gap2Idea, local context is supplied to the final LLM filter, which decides whether a previously selected candidate expresses the target statement.

\subsection{Recognition of limitations and future work}

Work on self-acknowledged limitations directly addresses one of our three targets. Kilicoglu et al.~\cite{kilicoglu2018automatic} formulate limitation recognition in clinical research as sentence-level binary classification and investigate both rules and learned classifiers. Their use of section headings and limitation zones provides a direct precedent for structural localisation. Lan et al.~\cite{lan2024automatic} subsequently annotate limitations in 200 RCT publications and separate detecting a limitation from assigning its type. Their fine-tuned PubMedBERT detector achieves a reported $F_1$ of 0.821 under their evaluation protocol. SAL supplies our human-labelled limitation benchmark, and the PubMedBERT model family supplies a supervised comparison. We distinguish that published score from our full-paper evaluation because the evaluated sentence pool and class balance affect precision and $F_1$.

Future-work recognition has a parallel history. Zhu et al.~\cite{zhu2019recognizing} use BERT-based recognition to identify future-research sentences in JASIST articles and cluster the extracted statements. The ACL FWS-RC corpus provides annotations and a taxonomy for future-work sentences in NLP papers~\cite{hao2020acl}. Using this resource, Zhang et al.~\cite{zhang2023fws} compare lexical and neural approaches, reporting their strongest binary recognition result with Bernoulli naive Bayes (macro-$F_1$ of 0.9073). Their recognition and direction-classification tasks are distinct, as are macro-$F_1$ and the positive-class $F_1$ used in our main evaluation. These published scores therefore provide context rather than a ranking against our paper-disjoint experiments.

Extracted statements also support analysis beyond detection. Suray et al.~\cite{suray2024future} examine how subsequent usable-security research engages with previously stated future work. More recently, Thaepprasit et al.~\cite{thaepprasit2026limits} analyse self-reported limitations in ACL and EMNLP papers through iterative human--AI qualitative coding. These studies show how author-written statements can support research-trend and reporting analyses. Gap2Idea addresses the preceding extraction step; it does not establish whether a stated gap remains unresolved or whether a proposed direction has subsequently been pursued.

\subsection{LLM extraction, inferred gaps and generated critique}

LLMs are increasingly used both to extract statements and to produce new assessments of research. BAGELS constructs limitation data from ACL, NeurIPS and PeerJ papers, supplements author-written material with external reviews, and studies retrieval-augmented limitation generation~\cite{alazher2025bagels}. FutureGen similarly uses extracted future-work material to support the generation and evaluation of new directions~\cite{alazher2025futuregen}. Cheng et al.~\cite{cheng2026future} directly investigate prompted LLM extraction of future-work sentences in NLP papers and connect the extracted material to keyword-based research-direction analysis. Their pipeline selects relevant sections before LLM processing. Together, these studies establish that both LLM extraction and structural selection already have close precedents.

Knowledge-gap identification also includes an inference task. GAPMAP explicitly distinguishes stated biomedical knowledge gaps from missing knowledge inferred from context, evaluating LLMs in paragraph-level and full-paper settings~\cite{salem2025gapmap}. This distinction is central to our scope: we select an existing sentence that articulates a gap and preserve its wording. We do not ask the model to construct an implicit gap that has no corresponding author-written statement.

Limitation generation and scientific critique involve a further change in the source of the output. LimGen studies suggestive limitation generation using 4068 ACL papers and their associated author-written limitations~\cite{faizullah2024limgen}. Despite its similar name, LimitGen is a separate benchmark for evaluating LLM-generated limitations and feedback on AI research papers~\cite{xu2025llms}. Tree-of-Concerns focuses specifically on unstated limitations through structured multi-agent critique~\cite{mishra2026tree}. These tasks can produce useful criticisms beyond those acknowledged by the authors. Our LLM instead returns a keep-or-drop decision for each candidate sentence; the resulting extraction remains attributable to the original text.

\subsection{Position of this work}

Our Stage~B builds on established methodology. Yin et al.~\cite{yin2019zeroshot} formulate zero-shot text classification as textual entailment, allowing a label to be expressed as a natural-language hypothesis. We apply this formulation to gap types using an existing NLI model. Here, zero-shot means that the classifier is not fitted on gap-detection training examples; the model has already been trained on NLI data, and our decision threshold is selected with labelled development data.

Our contribution is the evaluation of a particular combination: structural localisation, NLI candidate selection and a context-aware LLM filter with three precision--recall instructions. We examine how these stages interact, how the filter settings behave across datasets, and how the funnel compares with giving the same LLM the whole paper, in accuracy and cost, for LLMs of different sizes. Long inputs are a known difficulty for LLMs~\cite{liu2024lost}, and this comparison measures how much a filter in front of the LLM helps on full scientific papers. The evaluation covers both full papers and the section-level FWS resource, keeping their processing units and label sources explicit. Human-labelled limitation and future-work benchmarks support those two targets; findings for the joint three-type task remain provisional because that corpus is LLM-labelled. The resulting evidence concerns source-preserving extraction; assessing the quality of research ideas derived from these statements remains a separate task.
