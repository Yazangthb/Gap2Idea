Limitation
A weakness, caveat, bias or constraint the authors acknowledge about their own work (their study, method, model or data). Examples: small sample, no blinding, single setting, short follow-up, confounding, selection bias, limited generalizability, an unvalidated measure, a strong assumption, a failure mode. A sentence that states the cause of a limitation counts. A sentence that only states a consequence (for example, “This may have biased the results”) counts only if the cause is clear from it or from the sentence just before or after it.
Future work
An explicit direction the authors propose for future research.
Research gap
An unsolved problem, missing capability or open challenge the authors point to in the field or in prior work, not necessarily in their own study.
Not a gap
Everything else: background, methods, results, descriptions of related work, positive claims, recommendations for practice or policy, headers and fragments.
  • The highlighted text is one unit as our software split the paper. Sometimes it holds two sentences or a heading. Judge the whole highlighted text: answer Yes if any part of it states a gap.
  • Earlier work: a sentence that points out a shortcoming, missing capability or unsolved problem in existing methods or earlier studies is a Research gap, even if this paper then addresses it. A sentence that only reports what earlier work did or found, or a problem that earlier work has already solved, is Not a gap.
  • If it fits more than one type, choose Limitation first, then Research gap, then Future work.
